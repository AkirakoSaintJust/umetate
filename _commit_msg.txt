Add band-limited pulse (PWM) and triangle nodes

The band-limited oscillator trio is complete: saw, pulse, triangle. All share one
2nd-order polyBLEP residual (Tale / Martin Finke, "Making Audio Plugins Part 18").

- firmware/hal/fm1_math.h: fm1_poly_blep(p, inc, inv_inc) — the Q15 residual for a
  unit step at phase 0, extracted from the saw node (identical math). u = p/inc is
  the uint64 product `p * inv_inc` (no per-sample divide). The saw node now calls
  it (bit-identical; the spectral contract still passes).
- modules/nodes/pulse/: band-limited pulse/square. `width` (control, Q15, default
  0.5 = square) is the duty — the high fraction of the cycle — clamped to [0.01,
  0.99] so the two BLEPs never collide into a narrow-pulse artifact. Two BLEPs:
  +blep(ph) at the wrap, -blep(ph - pw) at the falling edge (`(ph + 1 - pw) mod 1`).
- modules/nodes/triangle/: the polyBLEP'd square (width 0.5) through a leaky
  integrator (one extra state). slope = inc/2^16 (block-derived) is the integration
  gain; the leak is a fixed integ>>10 per sample. This split (gain from leak, vs
  Finke's single increment coefficient) keeps the output full-scale at any pitch
  and avoids the >7 kHz instability; the cost is a brief note-on transient (~2^10
  samples) and a ~1 LSB DC offset. int64 accumulator peaks ~2^30 (no overflow).

docs/dsp-study.md §2.2 records the exact formulas + the leaky-integrator gain/leak
split rationale; the roadmap node table + backlog + §6.9 narrative mark the pulse
(was a backlog item) done and the trio complete.

Tests (host, spectral):
- pulse: non-silent-then-silent, and odd near-Nyquist harmonics (41..49) attenuated
  ~2x vs the naive square.
- triangle: non-silent-then-silent, full-scale peak, odd harmonics follow 1/n^2
  (3 ~ 1/9, 21 ~ 1/441) — the correct band-limited spectrum (the triangle already
  rolls off 1/n^2, so the claim is the right spectrum, not attenuation vs naive).
